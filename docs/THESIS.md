# Why V/MA

Written before any result; edit it with your own sector knowledge. Verify every dated fact yourself.

**Shared drivers.** Both are card networks that earn fees on payment volume and transactions, so the same
inputs move both: consumer spending, cross-border travel and e-commerce volume, FX volatility, and the same
regulatory and litigation exposure (interchange rules, merchant litigation, antitrust attention). Large relative
moves between them need a company-specific reason.

**Material differences (to check against filings).**
- Business mix: debit versus credit emphasis, revenue by region, and the size of value-added services each report.
- Reporting calendars and fiscal years differ, so earnings releases do not land on the same day.
- Company-specific legal or regulatory actions, M&A attempts, and network-level disputes.

**Why a divergence might persist.** A one-sided legal outcome, a lasting share shift, a structural regulatory
change for one network, or a different growth outlook. These create a new relationship; the formation-window hedge
ratio then describes the past, and the loss limit and z-stop exist for this case.

**Why it might revert.** Same end-demand, same business model, and arbitrage from investors who treat them as
substitutes. Most one-off earnings or news dislocations should be temporary.

**What the study can and cannot show.** It tests whether the spread's statistical behaviour was tradable net of
costs in the sample. It cannot prove why. Case studies list what was observable at the decision time (z, fit, half-life,
earnings dates if supplied); any explanation written afterwards is a hypothesis.

**Events to annotate in case studies** (verify dates and facts): earnings releases of each company, antitrust or
interchange actions against either network, the 2022 Russia suspension, and any merger attempts. For each loser,
write what would have invalidated convergence at the time.

**Expansion** beyond V/MA is justified only if the baseline is trustworthy and the economic link is stated
first (e.g. other networks or processors), not discovered from a correlation screen.
